Local Directory & Citation Management

Align the business details that appear across relevant directories, reduce avoidable conflicts, and create a more manageable source of truth.

Directory relevanceData consistencyNo phone required
Service overview

Make key sources agree.

Citation work is not a race to appear everywhere. It is a controlled process for relevant records, accurate information, and future maintainability.

01

Suitable for

Businesses with changed details, duplicate records, unclear directory ownership, inconsistent categories, or a need for a verified citation baseline.

02

Primary outcome

A reviewed set of relevant records aligned with approved business information and documented next actions.

03

Business input

The approved name, address or service-area policy, website, primary category, locations, previous identities, and available access.

04

Important limit

Third-party directories control their systems, review times, field formats, and whether a record can be amended or removed.

What is included

Relevant records, clear purpose.

01

Source-data confirmation

Agree the exact fields that directories should use and record known exceptions before outreach or editing begins.

02

Relevant directory review

Identify useful general, geographic, and sector-specific records without promising unnecessary listing volume.

03

Conflict and duplicate assessment

Separate true conflicts from harmless formatting differences, historic records, and platform-defined variations.

04

Correction and ownership actions

Update records where access and platform rules allow, and document items needing business verification or manual follow-up.

05

Consistency register

Keep a practical record of source details, directory status, known limitations, and future maintenance responsibility.

Process

One source to a clear network.

01

Confirm

Approve the source name, address policy, website, category, and location relationships.

02

Find

Review relevant records, likely duplicates, historic identities, and access conditions.

03

Align

Correct controllable conflicts and submit supported changes in a documented order.

04

Record

Track live, pending, blocked, and business-owned actions for future maintenance.

Directory consistency network

Compare identity fields.

Name consistency

One approved identity, with documented exceptions.

Directory names should reflect the real business name. Legal suffixes, location descriptors, and historic names need a deliberate rule—not improvised variation.

  • Approved public name
  • Legal-name context
  • Location naming rules
  • Historic-record notes
Address consistency

Describe the location model accurately.

Storefronts, offices, service-area businesses, and multi-location operators need different handling. The goal is correct location information, not forced uniformity.

  • Customer-facing address
  • Service-area policy
  • Suite and format rules
  • Location relationships
Website consistency

Send each record to the right owned destination.

The website route should be current, secure, useful to that location or business context, and free from unsupported tracking or redirect confusion.

  • Canonical destination
  • Location-level route
  • Redirect review
  • Link ownership
Business category

Keep directory classification genuinely relevant.

Category vocabularies differ by platform, so the closest accurate equivalent matters more than forcing identical wording everywhere.

  • Primary business meaning
  • Platform vocabulary
  • Relevant equivalents
  • No unrelated categories
Relevant measurements

Track records, not fake scores.

The useful output is a transparent register of reviewed sources and known actions. No fake percentages or directory-value claims are required.

Expectations and limitations

Consistency over uniformity.

Platforms format fields differently.

Abbreviations, category vocabularies, and address formats can vary without creating a material conflict.

Ownership may be unavailable.

Some records require business verification, legacy login recovery, paid platform access, or publisher support.

Updates can take weeks.

Review and publishing times belong to each directory and may not follow the same timeline.

Relevance is more useful than volume.

Nearloom does not create large numbers of unsupported listings simply to report a higher count.
