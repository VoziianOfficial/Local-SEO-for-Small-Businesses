Local SEO Audit & Action Plan

See what is accurate, incomplete, inconsistent, unhelpful, or unmeasured—then organise the work by impact, dependency, and business readiness.

Evidence-led reviewPrioritised actionsNo invented scores
Service overview

Find the next useful action.

An audit creates a baseline across the profile, directories, reviews, location content, website connections, and measurement access.

01

Suitable for

Businesses starting local SEO, changing provider, adding locations, recovering from inconsistent work, or unsure where to focus.

02

Primary outcome

A documented view of findings, dependencies, limitations, and prioritised actions—not a generic score.

03

Business input

Website and profile access, approved business data, target locations, service priorities, existing reports, and operational constraints.

04

Important limit

An audit identifies and prioritises work. Implementation is agreed separately unless explicitly included in scope.

What is included

Review controllable signals.

01

Business and access context

Confirm the operating model, approved details, locations, service priorities, platform access, and internal ownership.

02

Profile and citation review

Assess profile information, categories, services, duplicates, key directories, and material consistency conflicts.

03

Reviews and local content

Review request practices, response ownership, website service paths, and the usefulness of existing location pages.

04

Measurement readiness

Identify available profile, website, call, direction, and enquiry sources, plus important gaps or definition limits.

05

Prioritised action plan

Organise actions by importance, dependency, responsibility, evidence, and realistic sequence.

Process

Evidence to sequence.

01

Frame

Agree the locations, services, business model, access, known concerns, and decisions the audit must support.

02

Review

Inspect the available profiles, directories, website paths, reputation workflow, and data sources.

03

Prioritise

Separate urgent accuracy issues, foundational dependencies, useful improvements, and observation tasks.

04

Explain

Present the reasoning, owners, limitations, and next actions in language the business can use.

Priorities matrix

Five grounded lenses.

Accuracy

Is the visible information true now?

Accuracy issues deserve early attention because customers and platforms may rely on them immediately.

  • Approved identity
  • Location model
  • Hours and availability
  • Owned destinations
Completeness

Are useful fields and customer questions missing?

Completeness is judged by business fit and customer need, not by filling every available field without purpose.

  • Profile fields
  • Service coverage
  • Useful attributes
  • Decision information
Consistency

Do important sources agree materially?

The audit distinguishes meaningful conflicts from harmless formatting variations and platform-specific vocabularies.

  • Profile and website
  • Relevant directories
  • Location relationships
  • Historic records
Content

Does the website help someone make a local decision?

Service and location content should be useful, distinct, internally connected, and free from doorway-page tactics.

  • Service intent
  • Local relevance
  • Internal paths
  • Conversion clarity
Measurement

Can the business observe meaningful actions?

Profile, map, website, call, direction, and enquiry sources need clear definitions and ownership before they inform decisions.

  • Source access
  • Event definitions
  • Reporting context
  • Known data gaps
Relevant measurements

Measure decisions, not grades.

The deliverable shows evidence and state. Any future performance values come from verified business data after implementation.

Expectations and limitations

Audits create direction.

Access shapes depth.

Unavailable profile, analytics, directory, or website access may limit what can be verified directly.

Findings reflect the review date.

Platforms, competitors, listings, and business operations can change after evidence is collected.

Implementation needs agreement.

The action plan identifies work, but timing, ownership, cost, and execution depend on the final scope.

Outcomes remain variable.

No audit can guarantee search positions, calls, enquiries, visits, or revenue.
